# Seven-venue conference-country evidence brief

Frozen contract: v3  
Universe: 19,908 official papers; 19,907 enumerated records after one accepted
ICLR paper was deliberately left unresolved.

## Observation gates

- all seven venues exceed 90% country coverage;
- coverage ranges from 91.38% (NeurIPS) to 97.38% (ICLR);
- the 6.00 percentage-point spread passes the 15-point limit;
- the omitted ICLR record is 0.027% of its official universe.

## Brazil result

- full count: 27 papers;
- fractional count: 13.772024 papers;
- primary equal-venue fractional share: 0.0006402475 (0.0640%);
- primary rank in the frozen 16-country panel: 10th;
- secondary paper-weighted fractional share: 0.0007378924 (0.0738%);
- secondary rank: 10th.

Brazil trails Mexico in the Latin American comparison but ranks above
Argentina, Chile and Colombia. It also ranks above Indonesia, South Africa and
Turkey in the primary estimator.

## Sensitivity

Across equal-venue leave-one-venue-out estimates, Brazil's share ranges from
0.0005112546 to 0.0007469554. Its rank remains 10th in six scenarios and falls
to 12th when ICML is omitted. Under the paper-weighted estimator, Brazil is
usually 10th and rises to 9th when AAAI is omitted.

KDD ADS contributes no Brazil paper. ICML contributes the largest fractional
Brazil signal (4.5), followed by AAAI (3.333333), NeurIPS (2.642857), ICLR
(1.4625), EMNLP (1.333333) and ACL (0.5).

## Interpretation for the post

The result does not support a claim that Brazil currently has a broad
competitive advantage in top-conference AI research presence. It supports a
narrower statement: Brazil has visible but small participation, ranks second
among the Latin American countries in the frozen panel, and part of its
relative position depends on ICML. This is evidence about scientific presence,
not research quality, impact, specialization, talent stocks or conversion into
economic value.
